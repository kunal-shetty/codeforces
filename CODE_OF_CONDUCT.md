# Code of Conduct

Upsolve is built by people who like solving problems and helping others get better at it. We want everyone who takes part (beginners on their first 800, grandmasters, first-time contributors, long-time maintainers) to feel welcome.

## Our pledge

We pledge to make taking part in this project a harassment-free experience for everyone, whatever their age, body, disability, ethnicity, gender identity or expression, level of experience, education, nationality, appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

## What we expect

- Be kind and patient, especially with beginners. Nobody's rating says anything about their worth.
- Give and accept feedback on the work, not the person.
- Assume good intent, and ask before you assume.
- Credit other people's ideas and code.
- Own your mistakes and learn from them.

## What isn't acceptable

- Insults, put-downs or mocking someone's skill level ("that's a trivial div 4 problem, how can't you…").
- Harassment, in public or private, including unwelcome attention or contact.
- Sexualised language or imagery.
- Sharing someone's private information without their permission.
- Using this project to share solutions to **live** contests, or anything else that breaks a judge's rules.
- Anything else that would be inappropriate in a professional setting.

## Scope

This applies to every project space (issues, pull requests, discussions and reviews) and to anyone representing the project elsewhere.

## Enforcement

Report problems to the maintainers privately through a [GitHub security advisory](https://github.com/kunal-shetty/codeforces/security/advisories/new) (it works for conduct reports too), or by messaging a maintainer on GitHub. Every report is reviewed and kept confidential.

Maintainers may respond, depending on how serious the problem is, with:

1. **A note**: a private message explaining what was wrong.
2. **A warning**, with consequences for repeat behaviour.
3. **A temporary ban** from project spaces.
4. **A permanent ban.**
